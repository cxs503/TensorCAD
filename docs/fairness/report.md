| regular UV sphere | 2304 | 0.18 % | 100.0 % | 0.997857 | 0.2143 % |
| marching tets, raw | 15584 | 48.93 % | 376.7 % | 1.000490 | 0.0490 % |
| + taubin x30 | 15584 | 22.35 % | 261.7 % | 0.999645 | 0.0355 % |
| + projection x10 | 15584 | 1.42 % | 93.5 % | 1.000008 | 0.0008 % |

The area-weighted mean is the integral `(1/A) int H dA`, and the cotangent operator conserves it exactly on a closed mesh, which is why it survives the sliver field untouched while the pointwise value does not. This is the same sliver population `docs/mesh_conditioning.md` reports at 1.7 % of triangles: they carry almost no area and almost all of the pointwise error.

## 4. The parametric hull family is not G1 at its ends

`b(x) = B/2 * sin(pi x/L)^0.7` has `db/dx ~ x^-0.3`; the fit over `x/L = 1e-2 .. 1e-7` gives **-0.3000** (expected -0.3). The waterline therefore has an infinite slope at the stem and the stern, the surface is only C0 there, and every curvature-based quantity diverges: the first row of the table is the fitted data, and the last row is the value at `x/L = 1e-7`, 1.964e+01 1/m.

The crease count falls by more than half between the two grids, which is the warning this report exists to make: **a crease threshold applied to a coarse mesh measures the mesh, not the shape**. Fairness has to be judged on a converged grid or on the field, and the two hull-family statements above are the ones that survive refinement: the ends are not G1, and the keel line has unbounded curvature because the profile is `sqrt(z/T)`.
