# `tensorcad.intersect`: resolution contract and tie census

**Date**: 2026-11-08 · **Branch**: agent/m6465 · **Code under test**: `tensorcad/intersect.py`
(unchanged behaviour; one literal promoted to a named constant) ·
**Evidence**: [`audit.json`](intersect_resolution/audit.json),
[`fig_resolution.png`](intersect_resolution/fig_resolution.png) ·
**Generator**: `tools/intersect_resolution_audit.py`

## 1. Case / why this exists

`triangles_intersect` is the geometric gate behind the PC6 and ice-channel
services: `find_self_intersections` rejects a watertight mesh that folds through
itself, so a predicate that says "disjoint" when two faces actually touch lets a
folding hull through. Since the differential suite compares it against an exact
rational oracle, any red case there is treated as a defect until proven otherwise
— and the previous mission report (`docs/m6410_selfstart_verification.md`) left
`tests/test_intersect.py::TestExactDifferential::test_thirds_lattice_matches_halfspace_clipping`
red with 12 disagreements, calling it "a real defect … not fixed in this mission".

This page settles what those 12 (and the 27 that a 2000-pair sample finds) really
are, states the predicate's resolution as a contract, and pins it from both sides.

## 2. Method

Three exact-arithmetic instruments, all in `tests/intersect_oracle.py`, none of
them using floating-point arithmetic in the comparison:

| instrument | what it computes | independence |
|---|---|---|
| `triangles_intersect_exact` | edge piercing / plane-line interval intersection | reference route |
| `triangles_intersect_clip3d` | half-space clipping + plane-line interval | **shares the non-coplanar formulation since m6432** — a consistency check, not an independence proof (recorded in `ROUTES`) |
| `sub_resolution_gap` | exact closest distance (vertex-face + edge-edge candidates, `Fraction`) | exact for disjoint pairs; used as the contract-level classifier |
| `verdict_is_stable` | does the exact verdict flip under a ±2-ulp move of any single coordinate? | sufficient (not necessary) tie certificate |

Procedure: seeded random triangle pairs on two lattices — dyadic (every coordinate
binary-exact, so any disagreement is a logic error) and thirds (`-1, -1/3, 0, 1/3,
1`, so 1/3 is not representable and near-touches can land sub-ulp apart). Classify
every library-vs-oracle disagreement as **decided** (exact separation above
`RELATIVE_TOLERANCE * max(size, 1)`) or **sub-resolution** (below it). Then measure
the decision boundary directly by bisecting the gap of a blade tip above a plate at
four model scales and compare it with the declared band.

## 3. Results

### 3.1 Differential census (n = 2000 + 2000, seeded)

| lattice | samples | agree | disagree | decided | sub-resolution | lib `True`/exact `False` | lib `False`/exact `True` (missed contact) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| dyadic | 2000 | 2000 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| thirds | 2000 | 1973 | 27 | **0** | 27 | 27 | **0** |

* No decided disagreement in 4000 pairs ⇒ **no logic error detectable at double
  resolution**; the previous red test was a test-contract defect, not a code defect.
* Every sub-resolution disagreement runs in the conservative direction (contact
  claimed for a sub-band miss); the dangerous direction — a missed contact — is 0.
* The 27 sub-resolution gaps are 0.0116 / 0.0387 / 0.0589 ulp of the triangle size
  (min / median / max), i.e. the pairs differ in the last bit of their coordinates.

### 3.2 Measured resolution versus declared band

Bisection on a blade tip that stops `g` above a plate of edge length `s`:

| model scale [m] | edge length `s` [m] | measured boundary `g` [m] | `g/s` | declared band `RELATIVE_TOLERANCE·max(s,1)` [m] | measured / declared |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.001 | 0.00283 | 1.0000e-10 | 3.54e-08 | 1.0000e-10 | **1.000** |
| 0.01 | 0.02828 | 1.0000e-10 | 3.54e-09 | 1.0000e-10 | **1.000** |
| 1 | 2.828 | 2.8284e-10 | 1.000e-10 | 2.8284e-10 | **1.000** |
| 200 | 565.69 | 5.6569e-08 | 1.000e-10 | 5.6569e-08 | **1.000** |

So the predicate's resolution *is* the plane band, exactly: `1e-10 · max(s, 1 m)`.
Above 1 m it is relative (a 200 m hull resolves 5.7e-8 m); below 1 m the
`max(s, 1.0)` floor makes it an absolute 1e-10 m — deliberate (it keeps the band
meaningful for tiny parts) but it does mean the predicate is not scale-invariant
below 1 m, which the module docstring now states. The barycentric band
(`BARYCENTRIC_TOLERANCE = 1e-15`) is the narrower of the two and never binds.

### 3.3 Mesh-level controls

Clean octahedron (8 faces, all adjacent): 0 reported pairs. Two crossing sheets: 1
pair, `((0, 1),)`. Pruning unchanged: hash-grid result equals all-pairs result.

## 5. Pitfalls / limitations

1. **Agreement between two "exact" routes stopped being evidence.** m6432 repaired
   `triangles_intersect_clip3d` by making its non-coplanar branch intersect the same
   plane/plane line interval as `triangles_intersect_exact`. A shared logic error in
   that formulation would now be invisible to their agreement. Recorded as data in
   `intersect_oracle.ROUTES` and asserted by
   `TestResolutionContract::test_route_metadata_records_the_independence_loss`.
2. **Verdict stability is not a valid filter.** The corners-kissing pair
   (gap 3.0e-18 of its size) keeps its exact verdict under a 2^20-ulp move of every
   coordinate one at a time, because the *sign* of the separation is structural
   while its magnitude is negligible. It is 1 of the 27 counted as
   `stable_but_sub_resolution`; stability would have mislabelled it as a defect.
   Pinned by `test_stability_is_sufficient_but_not_necessary`.
3. **The distance meter is not a verdict.** `triangles_separation` is exact for
   disjoint pairs only; for intersecting pairs it can return a positive gap (an edge
   can pass through a face interior). Documented in its docstring.
4. Still open: the predicate is conservative by design, so downstream callers get a
   false alarm — never a missed fold — for sub-band pairs. Whether that is the right
   trade for `find_self_intersections` is a policy question for the mesh gate
   (`docs/mesh_export_gate.md`), not a property this audit can settle.

## 6. Error judgment against a reference

The predicate returns a boolean; there is no physical quantity to compare against
literature, so a percentage error like a hydrostatics benchmark cannot be quoted —
declared explicitly rather than glossed over. The comparable quantitative claims,
each against the exact rational oracle:

| claim | result | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| decided (above-resolution) disagreement rate, 4000 seeded pairs | 0 / 4000 = **0 %** | passes any tolerance |
| missed contacts (the unsafe direction) | **0** / 4000 | passes |
| sub-resolution disagreement rate, thirds lattice | 27 / 2000 = 1.35 %, all conservative, all within 0.059 ulp | expected by the contract |
| measured boundary / declared band, four scales | 1.000, 1.000, 1.000, 1.000 | exact agreement |
| mesh-level controls | octahedron 0, crossing sheets 1 | matches construction |

"Is every key quantity within 3 %?" — Yes: the two rates that must be zero are 0,
and the boundary ratio is 1.000 ± 2e-16 (float rounding of the bisection). The
1.35 % sub-resolution rate is not an error but the documented population of ties.

## 7. What changed in the code

* `tensorcad/intersect.py`: behaviour unchanged. The hardcoded `1e-15` in
  `_segment_pierces` became `BARYCENTRIC_TOLERANCE` (same value), and the module
  docstring now states the two resolution bands and the conservative contract.
* `tests/intersect_oracle.py`: added `verdict_is_stable`, `triangles_separation`,
  `relative_gap`, `sub_resolution_gap`, `longest_edge`, and the `ROUTES` map.
* `tests/test_intersect.py`: the thirds-lattice differential test now filters with
  the contract's own band (and bounds the tie population instead of silently
  skipping it); new `TestResolutionContract` pins both sides of the band, the
  recorded ulp tie, the stability limitation and the route metadata.
