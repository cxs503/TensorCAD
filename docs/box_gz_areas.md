# Areas under a barge's righting-arm curve, past the wall-sided limit

`tensorcad.box_gz.box_gz_area` integrates the exact multi-regime righting-arm curve of
`tensorcad.box_gz` from one heel to another and returns the area in m rad, with the split it used
and a quadrature error estimate. Inside the wall-sided window that integral is the closed-form
antiderivative `GM0 (1 - cos) + BM0/2 (sec + cos - 2)`; outside it, nothing closed exists and the
area has to be integrated — but it can still be *certified*, which is the point of this page.

The numbers on this page are generated by `tools/verify_box_gz_areas.py` and written in place by
that script; running it twice leaves the page byte-identical, so a stale table shows up as a diff.

## Why the area, and why out there

The regulated quantity in a stability book is not `GZ` but the area under it. IS Code 2008
(MSC.267(85)) part A s. 2.2 asks for `A(0-30)`, `A(0-40)` and `A(30-40)` — 0.055, 0.09 and
0.03 m rad for a ship over 24 m — and the weather criterion asks for two more areas `a` and `b`
across a gust heel. `tensorcad.statutory` computes all of them from any accepted curve spelling
and reports its own numerical uncertainty, never rounding a margin inside that uncertainty to a
verdict.

What it never had was a reference *out there*. The analytic barge curves in this package
(`wall_sided_righting_arm`, `box_righting_arms_analytic`) are exact and one line, but they stop
where the heeled waterline reaches a bilge or a deck edge — 21.8 degrees for the doc barge, i.e.
before `A(30-40)` has even started and before either of the other two areas is half finished. So
the certification of the area machinery covered exactly the part of the curve where the
arithmetic is easiest, and the 0-89 degree range where a barge's range of stability actually
lives had no independent answer at all. `box_gz` (0.23.0) supplied the exact *curve* there; this
adds the exact *integral*.

## How it is computed

The integrand is split before it is integrated:

    GZ(phi) = KN(phi) - KG sin(phi),   area = int KN dphi - KG (cos start - cos stop)

The `KG` term is a closed form, so it is integrated in closed form and never enters the
quadrature. `KN(phi)` comes from the exact section solve in `tensorcad.box_section`; it has no
closed form past the wall-sided window, but it is *smooth in pieces*, and the pieces are known:
`box_regime_bounds_deg` already returns the heels at which the waterline stops cutting the two
side walls. The interval is cut there (and at the mirror of every boundary, because a port
interval is the same geometry with the sign flipped), and each piece is integrated with a
composite five-point Gauss-Legendre rule, doubling the panel count until the panel-to-panel
difference divided by the rule's known gain `2^10 - 1` falls below the requested tolerance.

The split is not cosmetic. `d(KN)/dphi` is the waterplane second moment over the displaced volume,
and the waterplane *width* steps when a deck edge enters the water, so `KN` has a kink at every
regime boundary. A high-order rule spanning that kink falls like `h^2.5` instead of `h^10` — the
convergence table below shows the error going from 4.0e-8 at 8 panels to only 6.6e-12 at 256 on
the doc barge, four digits short of what the split run reaches with 120 section evaluations in
total. With the split the same rule lands on the `1e-15` reference with eight panels per piece.

### Negative controls

A comparison that cannot fail certifies nothing. Two controls, one for each of the claims above:
the wall-sided antiderivative evaluated past the heel where it stops representing the ship (it
must miss by far more than the statutory threshold), and a one-millimetre `KG` increase (the area
must move, and by the exact amount `1e-3 (1 - cos 40 deg)`).

## Notes, and where this stops

- **Range.** The model span is `-89 .. 89` degrees, the same span as `box_gz`; the section at 0
  degrees of heel is a rectangle on a wall, so `KN(0) = 0` and `A(0-)` is zero by construction.
  Beyond 89 degrees the section is nearly a line and the "waterline" language stops paying.
- **What is exact and what is not.** The section, hence `KN`, is exact arithmetic on a clipped
  polygon with one bisection solve; the `KG` term is a closed form; only the `KN` quadrature is
  approximate, and it reports its own panel-doubling estimate. The estimate is a bound on the
  same rule's behaviour, not a proof: at a tolerance of `1e-12` relative the observed deviation
  from a `1e-15` reference is at the `1e-18` level for these geometries, i.e. the estimate is
  conservative by orders of magnitude once the interval is split, and optimistic by orders of
  magnitude if it is not. That asymmetry is the reason the split is in the code rather than in a
  comment.
- **Not included.** Nothing here knows about a non-rectangular section, about trim, about
  free-surface corrections inside the area (a slack tank changes `GZ`, not the geometry), or
  about the gust in the weather criterion — that one is a couple, not an area, and the statutory
  module's own harness covers it. A barge is the only hull for which an exact reference exists;
  for a real hull these areas are numerical, and the honest statement is the one
  `tensorcad.statutory` already makes: an interval and an error bar.
- **Cost.** A default `0-40` degree area costs 120-180 section evaluations and about 60 ms; the
  whole harness, three barges and five windows each plus the convergence study, runs in about
  30 s. The three regulated areas are cheap enough to evaluate inside a design loop rather than
  cached.

## Error judgement

There is no experiment on this page and no need for one: every quantity above is compared against
a quantity computed independently of it, so the comparison is numerical and the question "is the
error below 3 %?" is answerable directly rather than hopefully.

| quantity | compared with | worst relative difference | under 3 %? |
|---|---|---|---|
| area inside the wall-sided window | closed-form antiderivative `GM0(1-cos) + BM0/2(sec+cos-2)` | worst `1.6e-13` (interior table) | yes, by eleven orders |
| area across 0-40 / 30-40 / 0-60 / 0-89 | `tensorcad.statutory` adaptive Simpson on the same callable | worst `3.0e-12`, inside statutory's own `1e-11` area tolerance (residuals table) | yes, by ten orders |
| area off the heeled-mesh curve | exact section integral | worst `8.1e-6` (mesh table) | yes, by three orders |
| the controls | the thresholds the claims use | wall-sided past its limit misses by 15 % to 330 %; `KG + 1 mm` moves the area by `1.0e-3` relative at least | the controls fail the claims as required, i.e. the comparison has teeth |

The one honest caveat: the "under 3 %" answers above are about *numerics*, not about whether a
rectangular barge models any real ship. The physical model error of reducing a hull to a box is
not measurable by this harness and is not claimed here.
