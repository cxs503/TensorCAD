# Three-directional offset-table fairing (GAP-CAD-C3-LINES-FAIRING)

A designer receives the lines as an **offset table** (half-breadths on stations ×
waterlines) and makes it fair by re-drawing every **waterline**, **station** and
**buttock**. `tensorcad/lines_fairing.py` automates that pass and — the part that makes it
usable as evidence — measures whether it worked.

    from tensorcad.lines_fairing import fair_offset_table, FairingSettings
    result = fair_offset_table(table, FairingSettings(offset_tolerance=2e-3))
    result.max_deviation          # <= offset_tolerance, by construction
    result.total_flips            # (input, pass 1, pass 2, ...) non-increasing
    result.flips["waterline"]     # per-family history (a family may pay, see below)
    result.table                  # a ShipOffsetTable, ready for the loft

## Acceptance and how it is met

| clause | how | where it is pinned |
|---|---|---|
| faired ordinates within a stated tolerance | every pass is projected onto `[y0 - tol, y0 + tol]`, `y >= 0`, and the run raises if the final deviation leaves the band | `test_tolerance_band_is_respected` |
| curvature sign reversals fall monotonically, ≥ 2 hulls | a pass is committed only if `(total flips, total bending energy)` improves; a worse sweep is retried at half relaxation and then refused | `test_flip_history_is_monotone_non_increasing` |
| comparison with a hand-fairing reference | the analytic half-breadth surface of each formula hull (ground truth) plus an independent local-quadratic smoother | `test_hand_fairing_reference_agrees_within_the_tolerance`, `test_fairing_moves_a_scattered_table_towards_the_analytic_surface` |

Sign reversals are counted on the discrete circumscribed-circle curvature, ignoring values
below `curvature_floor` (2 %) of the curve's maximum `|kappa|`, so round-off on a straight
run is not mistaken for an inflection.

## The kernels, and why `local` is the default

* `local` — moving least-squares quadratic over a 5-point stencil, *shape-masked*: a point
  whose local residual exceeds `shape_threshold` (3) robust standard deviations is treated
  as design (a knuckle, a turn of the bilge) and left where the designer put it.
* `energy` — the classical smoothing spline: the largest third-derivative weight whose
  solution stays in the band (bisection; banded Cholesky, `O(n p²)`). Quadratics sit in its
  null space, so a clean Wigley table is a fixed point of it too.

Measured (see `docs/lines_fairing/results.json`, regenerated by
`PYTHONPATH=. python examples/lines_fairing/run_example.py`): the energy kernel is the more
accurate of the two on quadratic curves (Wigley), and the *less* accurate on sections that
are steeper than the grid resolves (Series 60, tanker class), because a global third
derivative cannot tell a genuine near-step from scatter. That is why `local` is the default
and why both are kept: the comparison is the evidence.

## What the numbers say (and what they do not)

* RMS distance to the analytic half-breadth surface falls on the Wigley and Series 60
  tables, and so does the reversal count; the *maximum* error can grow up to the tolerance
  band on a few stiff points, which is the price of the band being the constraint.
* On the full-scale tanker class at a coarse 17-waterline grid the local kernel makes the
  RMS **worse**: its section is `y ~ z**(1/24)`, a near-step that 17 waterlines cannot
  resolve, so the local quadratic is biased at the first off-keel point. At 33 × 25 the same
  run improves it. This is asserted as a caveat in the test suite, not smoothed over: the
  fairing is only meaningful where the grid resolves the hull.
* The guarantee is on the **total** reversal count. One family may pay for another: on the
  tanker class the waterline family (0 reversals to begin with) gains 7 while the station
  family loses 34. The per-family histories are in the result so that trade stays visible.
* A buttock is a *derived* contour, so its ordinate budget is `buttock_band_fraction`
  (25 %) of the tolerance; corrections that would make a section non-monotone in `z` are
  refused and counted (`monotone_refusals`).
* No topology change, no bulb/transom patch handling, no deck-edge or centre-plane
  fairness beyond what the table holds. There is no human designer's marked-up table in
  this repository, so "hand fairing" is the analytic surface plus an independent smoother.
