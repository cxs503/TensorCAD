## Three reconciliation cases (new, on neither side)

The two stability implementations are only one module if they agree numerically, so
`tests/test_stability.py` gained:

1. `test_plane_cut_and_flux_identity_agree_on_the_hull_waterplane` — the plane-cut
   Green integration and the wetted-flux identity measure `A`, `I_T`, `I_L` to
   **round-off** (measured 0.0, 2.2e-16, 1.6e-15 relative) on two grids. Two different
   theorems over two different sets of faces agreeing is evidence they see the same
   waterplane, not two nearby ones.
2. `test_the_two_stability_routes_report_the_same_metacentric_height` — KB, KM_T and
   GM_T identical to round-off between `initial_stability(mesh, kg=0.6)` and
   `initial_stability_from_hydrostatics(hydro, height_of_gravity=0.6)`, and both within
   3 % of the analytic surface (so agreement is not agreement on a shared error).
3. `test_split_hull_reports_no_waterplane_inertia_instead_of_an_off_axis_one` — the
   split hull reports `nan` and the hydrostatics-first block refuses it, rather than
   quoting an inertia about an axis the designer did not ask for.

## Known limitations (honest)

* The two implementations are kept, not unified: `initial_stability` (mesh-first)
  needs a mesh and handles heeled/multi-loop sections; the hydrostatics-first variant
  needs a `Hydrostatics` record and is what the closed forms feed. A future release
  could make the first call the second internally, but collapsing them in a *cure*
  merge would have changed an API under the branch's author.
* Inertias are not reported for the fore-aft split hull (see `hull.py`): the
  reference moments are about midship, and converting them needs `LCF` plus a
  parallel-axis correction that neither side had implemented. Reporting `nan` is
  explicit; inventing the correction inside a merge was judged out of scope.
