# Heeled righting arm on the transom-stern hull family

**Mission** m7551 (selfstart, infrastructure seat) · **Module under test**
`tensorcad/transom.py` + `tensorcad/gz.py` + `tensorcad/righting_arm.py` · **Code
changed** none in the solvers (verification-only mission) · **Tests**
`tests/test_transom_gz_verification.py` (11 cases) · **Artifacts**
`artifacts/transom_gz/evidence.json`, `fig1_gz_routes.png`, `fig2_consistency.png`,
`fig3_waterline.png` (all written by `tools/verify_transom_gz.py`).

Closes a named verification debt: the GZ chain was anchored on a box barge (wall-sided
closed form), a circular section (`GM sin phi`) and the Wigley hull (symmetric, fine
stern). It had **never** run on a hull whose aft body ends in an **immersed transom
plate** -- the form every real displacement vessel above `Fn ≈ 0.25` has, and the one
family in this repository that is not a benchmark body. The Wigley gap was closed by
m6594 (`docs/wigley_gz_verification_report.md`); this is the same class of work on a
ship-like body.

---

## 1. Case

`generate_transom_hull()` at its default parameters -- a closed, watertight solid whose
stern is a planar plate at `x = 0` and whose aft breadth is cut from a longer parent
body (`transom_fraction = 0.2`). The hull is generated **to its design waterline**, so
before it can be heeled at all it needs a topside: `gz.with_freeboard` removes the
waterline cap, extrudes the boundary ring vertically by `freeboard`, and re-closes it
with a deck fan.

| quantity | value |
|---|---|
| `L × B × T` (m) | 10.0 × 2.0 × 0.8 |
| transom fraction `τ` | 0.20 (parent body `L_parent = 12.5 m`) |
| grid | `nx = 41`, `nz = 13` → 1013 vertices, 2022 faces |
| keel datum | `z = −0.8` |
| freeboard band | 0.15 m (deck at `z = +0.15`) |
| `KG` (centre of gravity above keel) | 0.30 m |
| heel angles | 0, 2, 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 40 deg |

Mesh vs its own closed form (the family's analytic hydrostatics):
`V_mesh = 8.216624 m³` against `V_analytic = 8.281657 m³` → **−0.785 %** at this grid.
Initial stability from the waterline section of the *unbanded* mesh: `KB = 0.483005 m`,
`BM_T = I_T/∇ = 3.898926/8.216624 = 0.474517 m`, therefore
**`GM = KB + BM_T − KG = 0.657522 m`**.

## 2. What was done

1. Banded the hull with `with_freeboard(mesh, 0.15)`; asserted the upright displacement
   is unchanged (the band is above the waterline) and that the mesh stays closed.
2. Ran **two independent integrators** over the same heel sweep:
   * `gz.righting_arm_curve` -- per-triangle clipping, analytic waterplane integrals,
     inner sweep on the waterline level;
   * `righting_arm.righting_arm_curve` -- section curves on a rotated frame, bisection
     on the level, `GZ = y_B cos φ + (z_B − z_G) sin φ`.
   They share no code path, so their agreement is evidence rather than a tautology.
   They even report the buoyancy centre in different frames (body frame from the keel
   versus world frame), which is why only the **arm** is compared.
3. Checked the constant-displacement condition at every heel on both routes.
4. Checked the small-angle limit `GZ(φ)/sin φ → GM` against the waterplane-moment GM.
5. Measured the model's own validity bound -- deck-edge immersion
   `atan(freeboard / half_breadth) = atan(0.15 / 1.0) = 8.531°` -- and limited the
   physical claim to it.
6. Traced the nonzero upright arm to its actual cause instead of reporting "≈ 0".

Tool chain: pure Python, no external solver; the artifact and the three figures are
regenerated by `python3 tools/verify_transom_gz.py`, and the artifact is re-checked
against the test module's own arithmetic (`test_the_committed_artifact_matches_today_s_arithmetic`)
so a stale file cannot pass.

## 4. Error judgement

| quantity | reference | measured | error | threshold | verdict |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| `V` at upright | analytic 8.281657 m³ | 8.216624 m³ | **−0.785 %** | < 3 % | PASS |
| `KB` | mesh `initial_stability` | 0.483005 m | — (same mesh) | — | reference |
| `GM` | `KB + BM_T − KG` | 0.657522 m | identity | 1e-12 rel | PASS |
| `GZ(2°)/sin 2°` | `GM = 0.657522 m` | 0.651889 m | **−0.857 %** | < 3 % | PASS |
| `GZ(10°)/sin 10°` | `GM` | 0.628198 m | −4.47 % | not a criterion | expected curvature |
| `V(φ)` vs `V(0)` | constant displacement | ≤ 7.03e-13 rel | — | < 1e-9 | PASS |
| two routes | each other | 1.41e-13 m | — | < 1e-9 m | PASS |
| upright arm | 0 (symmetric hull) | **4.0165e-5 m** | 2.0e-5 of `B` | < 1e-4 m | PASS (flagged, §5.1) |

**Is the key quantity within 3 %?** Yes: the small-angle righting arm reproduces the
metacentric height to **0.86 %** at 2 deg, and the displacement the whole chain rests on
is within **0.785 %** of the family's closed form. No quantity in the table is claimed
beyond 3 % except the deliberately listed `GZ(10°)` entry, which is not a small-angle
comparison at all but the curvature of a real GZ curve.

## 5. Notes, limitations, and the one defect found

### 5.1 The upright arm does not vanish exactly, and it is the mesh, not the integrator

`GZ(0) = 4.0165e-5 m` on a hull that is port/starboard symmetric. Both integrators
return the **same** number to 1e-17, and the raw (unbanded) transom mesh has a
transverse centre of buoyancy of `+4.0165227719934e-5 m` -- identical to the arm. The
cause is the mesh: the lofted quads between stations are **not planar**, so splitting
each quad along a consistent diagonal produces a solid that is not exactly
mirror-symmetric even though every vertex has an exact mirror partner (checked: 0
mispaired vertices at 1e-12). The artifact is 2e-5 of the beam, four orders below the
grid's own discretisation error, but it is a real (open) generator defect and is
recorded as such rather than rounded away: a symmetric quad split (or an alternating
diagonal) would remove it. **Not fixed in this mission** -- it is a `transom.py`
geometry change, outside a verification-only scope, and the honest statement is that
the hull generator is only *approximately* symmetric.

### 5.2 The physical claim stops at deck-edge immersion

`with_freeboard` adds a **vertical** wall, so above `8.531°` the dipping deck edge is
immersed and the model's topside is a surrogate, not the real flare. The GZ numbers
above 8.5 deg are still the exact arms *of the banded body* (that is what the tests
assert), but they are not a prediction for a real ship's 30-deg arm. The mission that
anchored the Wigley GZ curve compared slope at 5 deg with a 0.2 m band, whose bound is
about 21.8 deg -- inside, but a reader should know the bound exists. A hull with
freeboard in the parameterisation is the real fix.

### 5.3 Other limits

* Transverse heel only; coupled trim/heel equilibrium is not exercised (a "free to
  trim" solve is still absent from the library, see README).
* One `KG`; no free-surface correction, no damage, no deck cargo shift.
* One hull (`τ = 0.2`, default grid). A `τ`-sweep would strengthen the claim that the
  transom plate is what the integrators see, but the analytic hydrostatics of the
  family already tie the geometry side down (`docs/transom_stern.md`).
* Architecture: pure Python, loong64, no external solver involved; the arithmetic is
  sequence-ordered summation over triangles, so the 1e-13 agreement level is
  architecture-dependent in its last digits but not in its verdict.
