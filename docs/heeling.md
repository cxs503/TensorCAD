# Large-angle stability: `GZ(phi)` and the cross curves `KN`

`docs/stability.md` stops where `GM` stops: at the upright position. `GM` is the slope of the
righting-arm curve at zero heel, and a ship with a healthy `GM` still capsizes if the curve
peaks early and dies. `tensorcad.heeling` computes the curve itself, so that a loading
condition can be judged by its **range of stability** and its **maximum righting arm**
rather than by a single number measured at the only angle where the ship is not heeling.

Everything below is regenerated by

```bash
python examples/heeling/run_example.py --output docs/heeling
```

which exits non-zero if any of its ten claims stop holding. The tables are read straight out
of `docs/heeling/results.json` written by that run; the figure is `docs/heeling/plot.png`.

## 1. What is computed

Heel the ship by `phi` and let it find the waterline that displaces the loading condition's
volume. With `B` the centroid of that volume and `G` the centre of gravity, both expressed
in the **heeled body frame**,

    GZ(phi) = cos(phi) (y_B - y_G) + sin(phi) (z_B - z_G),

    KN(phi) = cos(phi) y_B + sin(phi) z_B,          GZ = KN - KG sin(phi) - y_G cos(phi).

`KN` depends on the hull and the displacement alone, which is why one cross-curve table
serves every `KG`: that is what `cross_curve`, `cross_curves` return, and the identity above
is asserted pointwise by `tests/test_heeling.py`.

Two properties fix the sign convention and are asserted rather than described:

* `GZ(0) = y_B - y_G = 0` for a hull symmetric about its centreline;
* `dGZ/dphi (0) = GM`.

## 2. How the submerged volume is measured

Not by sampling and not by a strip sum. The closed surface is clipped against the heeled
waterplane triangle by triangle, and the volume and its first moment come from the
divergence theorem,

    V     = (1/3) * closed_integral (p . n) dS
    V * c = (1/4) * closed_integral p (p . n) dS

with the origin placed **on the cutting plane**. That placement is the trick: the cap that
closes the clipped solid has `p . n = 0` over its whole area, so it contributes nothing to
either integral and never has to be assembled into polygon loops -- no chaining, no
tolerance-based vertex matching, no cap triangulation. The clipped triangles are integrated
in closed form (the three-point vertex rule is exact for the quadratic `p (p . n)`), so the
only error in the result is the error already in the mesh.

The waterline offset is then solved for the target volume. `V(offset)` is non-decreasing
because a plane sweeping through a solid encloses a non-decreasing volume, so the root is
bracketed by the body's own vertex projections and found by Illinois (regula falsi with a
halved stale end): about twelve volume evaluations, where halving the bracket would take
forty-seven.

Two consequences are worth being explicit about:

* **Constant displacement, not constant waterline.** A `GZ` curve is defined at fixed
  displacement, so the offset is re-solved at every heel angle. The measured volume drift
  across the curved hull's curve is 7.11e-14 relative.
* **A hull capped at its waterline has no `GZ` curve.** The repository's hull families are
  lofted to the design waterline and closed there, which is exactly right for hydrostatics
  and exactly wrong for heeling: the displaced volume at the design draught already equals
  the whole solid, so every heel angle would need the entire body under water.
  `waterline_offset` raises `HeelError` in that case instead of returning nonsense, and the
  curved-hull evidence below floats the mesh at 70 % of its design draught so that the hull
  above the waterline is genuine freeboard.

## 3. The reference: a wall-sided barge, exact at every angle

A rectangular barge is the one body whose `GZ` is known in closed form *away* from upright.
Its section between `z = 0` and `z = T + y tan(phi)` has area `2bT` independent of `phi` (the
linear term integrates out), hence the floating draught does not change with heel and

    y_B = tan(phi) b^2 / (3T),   z_B = T/2 + b^2 tan^2(phi) / (6T),   BM = b^2/(3T),

so that `GZ = sin(phi) (GM + BM tan^2(phi)/2)` -- the wall-sided formula, exposed as
`wall_sided_righting_arm`. It holds while the waterline stays on both vertical sides: above
the bilge and below the deck edge.

Geometry: `L = 10.0 m`, `B = 2.0 m`, `D = 1.5 m`, `T = 0.8 m`,
`KG = 0.5 m`, `KB = 0.4`, `BM = 0.416666667`, `GM = 0.316666667 m`,
displacement `16.000000 m^3`. A parallelepiped mesh *is* the parallelepiped, so
this table isolates the kernel: anything above round-off would be a bug in the clip, the
integrals or the arm.

Deck-edge immersion is at `phi = atan((D-T)/(B/2)) = 34.99 deg`; past it the

exactly and `GZ = D/2 - KG` = 0.550000000000 m; the kernel returns
`KN = 0.750000000000` m.

## 4. The headline cross-check: `dGZ/dphi` at zero is `GM`

The slope is taken from the curve itself by a central difference, and compared with
`GM = KB + BM - KG` assembled from **upright** geometry only: `BM = I_T / volume` with `I_T`
from `tensorcad.stability.waterplane_moments`, which cuts the waterplane and integrates it
round its own loops with Green's theorem. None of that touches a heeled equilibrium, so the
agreement is a real check on the clip, the centroid, the solve and the arm.

| step [deg] | dGZ/dphi | GM [m] | difference [m] | observed order |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.8 | 0.316696997083 | 0.316666666667 | +3.033e-05 | 2.00 |
| 0.4 | 0.316674248510 | 0.316666666667 | +7.582e-06 | 2.00 |
| 0.2 | 0.316668562080 | 0.316666666667 | +1.895e-06 | 2.00 |
| 0.1 | 0.316667140517 | 0.316666666667 | +4.739e-07 | nan |

The difference is second order in the step, as a central difference of a smooth odd curve
must be -- which is itself evidence that the curve is smooth and that no step-size accident
is hiding in the agreement.

## 6. Discretisation: the curved hull's own error

The kernel is exact for a polygon mesh, so the remaining error is the chord error of a curved
hull's sides. Measured by refining the grid at 10 deg of heel:

| grid | faces | GZ(10 deg) [m] | difference from 161x41 [m] | relative |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 21x9 | 678 | 0.045833897 | -1.60e-04 | 3.48e-03 |
| 41x13 | 1998 | 0.045939910 | -5.39e-05 | 1.17e-03 |
| 81x25 | 7838 | 0.045983883 | -9.90e-06 | 2.15e-04 |
| 161x41 | 25918 | 0.045993787 | +0.00e+00 | 0.00e+00 |

Monotone decrease, and the default 41x13 grid is already within
1.2e-03 of the finest -- the heeled *displaced volume* averages the chord
error rather than amplifying it. For comparison the same grid is 0.24 % on the upright
displacement volume in `docs/wigley.md`.

## 8. Limits of this module

* **No free-surface correction.** A tank with a free surface raises the effective `G` by
  `sum(i_x) / displacement`. `GZ` here is the *solid* arm, as `GM` in `docs/stability.md` is
  the solid `GM`; the two documents are consistent about that.
* **No trim coupling.** The waterline is held parallel to the design waterline: heel only, no
  simultaneous trim, so a hull whose LCB shifts at heel would need the full two-parameter
  equilibrium solve.
* **No damage or flooding cases**, no class-rule criteria (`IMO A.749`, `IS Code 2008`), and no
  righting-energy (`e^40`) computation -- the curve these would consume is what is new here.
* **Freeboard is required.** A mesh capped at the waterline cannot be heeled; see section 2.
  Lofting the hull families to a deck edge is the clean fix and is not part of this change.
* **The cap area is a by-product, not a full waterplane.** `waterplane_area` at heel is the
  area of the cutting plane inside the hull, which is what a `GZ` calculation needs; it does
  not report the second moments at heel.
