## 1. Case: what the family had, and what it lacked

The Series 60 parametric family in `tensorcad/series60.py` reproduces the published
parent-form integrals to round-off but ended both hulls -- the real one had a
bulbous bow and often a transom, the model had a point at each end. This audit adds
both ends as *parameters*, then checks that they do not disturb what the family
already promised, and regenerates the five-Cb gradient (Cb = 0.60-0.80) against the published
particulars.

* `L = 10.0 m`, `B = 1.5 m`, `T = 0.6 m`, water 1025.0 kg/m^3,

## 2. Method

* `transom_ratio` blends the aft deck taper, `f(xi) = (1-tau) f_base(xi) + tau`,
  which is the only blend that keeps the midship station full and lifts the aft end
  to `tau`. Every moment the closed forms need stays affine or quadratic in `tau`,
  so the transom is analytic; the loft also gains a real stern face, otherwise the
  mesh would not close.
* `bulb_*` places **half** a spheroid of revolution ahead of the forward
  perpendicular (`A_BT = pi b^2`, Holtrop & Mennen's transverse bulb area). Half,
  because the rest would sit inside a hull whose displacement is already integrated.
* Two independent references per new feature: closed form against a second
  parameterisation (horizontal slabs for the bulb; the legacy integrals for the
  transom), and both against the exported mesh.
* Predeclared bars: published comparison `3 %`,
  mesh against own closed form `2 %`.

`tau` is a *breadth* ratio -- the stern half-breadth over `B/2` -- because that is
the quantity the loft can honour exactly. The area ratio a resistance calculation
wants, `A_T / A_m`, is one column further right and is smaller by the ratio of the
stern section coefficient to the midship one (0.74 at `Cb = 0.70`). Reporting both
is the difference between a parameter and a wish.

Two things to notice: the integrals do not move while `n_aft` and the taper gain
do (a transom redistributes sections at constant `Cb`, `Cwp` and `LCB`, which is
what a transom physically does), and the mesh's own stern area reproduces the
closed form to a few tenths of a percent -- that check is what caught the first
version of `transom_area`, which was half the section area because port and
starboard were counted once.

## 5. Notes, limits, and what this does not say

* The bulb is a **separate closed solid**, not boolean-unioned with the hull:
  `metadata['appendages']['bodies'] == 2`. Totals add its closed-form displacement,
  which is exact, but a solver that needs one watertight body will have to union it.
* `tau` is a breadth ratio (see 3.2). A user who sets `tau = 0.2` gets
  `A_T/A_m = 0.148`, and the metadata reports the achieved value next to the input.
* The mesh volume of the hull itself converges from below at first order in the
  vertical spacing (-0.94 % volume at 161x41), inherited from the
  `zeta^(1/m)` section: it is not a defect of the new ends.
* Nothing here validates resistance, stability or seakeeping of a hull with a bulb
  or transom -- only that the geometry and its hydrostatics are what they claim.

close at round-off and the largest published deviation anywhere is +0.38 %, which is the source table's own
`Cb = Cp * Cm` residual rather than an implementation error. The comparison is
capable of failing -- the uncoupled control misses `Cwp` by 10.72 % -- so a small residual means something.

The largest number anywhere in the new geometry is the exported hull mesh's own volume deficit against its closed form at 161 x 41: +1.21 %,
the known first-order convergence of the `zeta^(1/m)` loft. It is reported rather
than tuned, and it is why the published comparison runs on closed forms.
