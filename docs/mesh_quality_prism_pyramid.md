# Prisms and pyramids in the mesh-quality library: the two volume cells that were missing

*Mission:* `GAP-CAD-D1-MESH-QUALITY-LIB` (selfstart m19964, proposer seat).
*Code:* `tensorcad/meshquality/metrics.py` (`prism_metrics`, `pyramid_metrics`),
`tensorcad/meshquality/reference.py` (`reference_prisms`, `reference_pyramids`),
`tensorcad/meshquality/report.py` (`DEFAULT_LIMITS`), `tensorcad/meshquality/io.py`.
*Evidence:* `artifacts/mesh_quality_prism_pyramid/audit.json`, `quality.png`,
`manifest.json` (sha256), regenerated by `python tools/prism_pyramid_audit.py`
(exit 1 on any failed criterion). *Tests:* `tests/test_meshquality_prism_pyramid.py` (21).

## 1. Why these two shapes

The library measured `tri`, `quad`, `tet` and `hex`. A ship CFD mesh is hex-dominant with
**prism layers** on the hull and **pyramids** wherever a hex/tet transition needs a
conforming face — both shapes reach a solver without ever passing through the four kinds
above, and a gate that silently measures nothing for them is worse than no gate, because
its PASS looks the same.

The acceptance for this finding is *"metrics from the same definitions as the reference
implementations, on ≥3 meshes, difference < 1e-6 or explained"*. This host still has no
`gmsh`, no `vtk` and no installable `meshmagick` (`pip` index is the Loongnix mirror; both
`meshmagick` and `gmsh` resolve to "no matching distribution"), so the parity route taken
here is the same one the triangle/hex work took: **transcribe the definitions, then check
the transcription against a second implementation built on different arithmetic**, and
state plainly that it is not a diff against the upstream binaries.

## 2. Definitions, and where "ideal" had to be decided

Both cells use the standard isoparametric maps in Gmsh/VTK node order (prism: nodes 0-2 on
one triangular face, 3-5 on the other, 3 above 0; pyramid: base quad 0-3, apex 4). Metrics:

| metric | prism | pyramid |
|---|---|---|
| `volume` | sum of the signed volumes of the conforming 3-tet split | sum of the conforming 2-tet split |
| `min_angle` / `max_angle` | over the 2 triangular + 3 quadrilateral faces | over the base quad + 4 side triangles |
| `skewness` | `max(equiangle(tri faces, 60°), equiangle(quad faces, 90°))` | `max(equiangle(base, 90°), equiangle(sides, 60°))` |
| `scaled_jacobian` | `min` over the 6 corners of `det J / Π|column|` | `min` over the 4 base corners + base centre |
| `min_sicn` | `3 |det J|^(2/3)/||J||_F²` at the 6 corners, each normalised by the same corner of the ideal prism | same, at the 5 sampled points, normalised by the ideal pyramid |
| `edge_ratio`, `min_edge`, `max_edge` | over the 9 edges | over the 8 edges |

Three decisions are worth more than the formulas:

1. **The pyramid apex is excluded, and that is not a shortcut.** Every base shape function
   carries a `(1-ζ)` factor, so `∂/∂ξ = ∂/∂η = 0` at the apex and `det J = 0` there for
   *every* pyramid that can exist. Measured: the ideal pyramid's apex determinant is
   exactly `0.0` (`tests/test_meshquality_prism_pyramid.py::test_pyramid_apex_is_excluded_because_it_is_singular`).
   A metric that sampled the apex would report 0 for a perfect element.
2. **`min_sicn` is normalised point-wise against the reference element**, so the ideal
   prism (equilateral base, unit height) and the ideal pyramid (unit square base, apex at
   unit height) both read **1.0**. This is the tet precedent (`_tet_corner_icn`), and it
   matters: the raw expression reads `0.9086` for the ideal prism and `0.4714` for the
   ideal pyramid, which would make the shipped `min_sicn >= 0.1` limit mean two different
   things for two cell kinds.
3. **Equiangle skewness has no ideal square pyramid at unit height.** A right square
   pyramid's side-face apex angle is `2 asin(0.5/√1.5) = 48.19°`, not 60°, so the *ideal*
   pyramid scores **skewness 0.1968**, and the zero-skew shape is the pyramid of height
   `1/√2` — a flatter pyramid than any mesher builds. Consequence: skewness is **not**
   gated for pyramids (and not for prisms either — a square-footprint prism column has
   45-45-90 triangular faces and therefore skewness 0.25 while being a perfectly good
   boundary-layer cell). `min_sicn` is the metric that carries the gate for these kinds.

`scaled_jacobian` is deliberately *not* renormalised: the ideal prism reads **0.8660254**
(= sin 60°, one corner has non-orthogonal edges) and the ideal pyramid **0.8164966**
(= √(2/3)), exactly as the regular tetrahedron reads 0.7071068. The gate limit (0.1) and
the documented ideal values are in `docs/mesh_quality.md`'s units, not rescaled.

## 3. Method: the second route

`reference_prisms` / `reference_pyramids` recompute every column by **different
arithmetic**: the Jacobian is a central finite difference of the isoparametric map
(`h = 1e-5`) instead of its analytic derivative, volumes come from Cayley-Menger
determinants of the tet split instead of triple products, and angles from
`atan2(|u×w|, u·w)` instead of `acos` of the normalised dot product. The sample points,
edge tables and face tables are duplicated in the reference module on purpose, so it
cannot inherit a mistake from the tables it is meant to check. The ICN normaliser is
evaluated through the *same* finite-difference route, so it cancels rather than being
taken on trust.

**Answer to the acceptance question, per item:** every metric agrees with the second route
to **< 1e-9** (the acceptance is 1e-6); volumes agree with the closed-form geometric
volume of the shape to **exactly 0** (the prism plate and the cube fan sum to
`1.4 = 2×2×0.35` and `1.0` in double precision); the ideal elements match their closed
forms to **< 1e-12** on all seven metrics (`ideal_element_failures` is empty in the
artifact). So the **<3 %**-style question is not the right frame here — the quantities are
algebraically identical, not approximately equal, and the largest residual is `6.2e-10`
relative on a *degenerate* cell, i.e. 1e-8 of the 1 % band. What is *not* established is
parity with `gmsh`/`meshmagick` binaries (§6).

Gate failures, with the deciding measure:

| mesh | failing check (limit, measured) |
|---|---|
| `collapsed_prism_layer` | `prism.min_sicn` ≥ 0.1, got 0.0 (1 non-positive cell); `prism.scaled_jacobian` ≥ 0.1, got 0.0; `prism.min_angle` ≥ 10°, got 0.0 |
| `inverted_pyramid_fan` | `pyramid.min_sicn` ≥ 0.1, got −0.839947 (6 non-positive); `pyramid.scaled_jacobian` ≥ 0, got −1.0 |

Two of those deserve to be called findings rather than smoke tests:

* **F1 — the winding of a pyramid is not the winding of a hexahedron face, and the metric
  says so.** The cube-centre fan was first written with the six base quads wound the way a
  hexahedron's faces are wound (outward). All six pyramids then came out at
  `scaled_jacobian = -1.0`, `min_sicn = -0.8399`, with *every other* metric identical to
  the correct mesh (`edge_ratio` 1.1547, `skewness` 0.08774, volume 1/6 each). A library
  that clamped signs, or reported `abs()` volume only, would have passed that mesh. The
  base of a pyramid must be wound counter-clockwise **seen from the apex**, which is the
  reverse of a hexahedron face — recorded here because it is the first thing a
  hand-written pyramid test gets wrong.
* **F2 — a real boundary layer sits close to the `min_sicn` limit.** The three-layer prism
  plate (heights 0.05/0.10/0.20 over unit columns, aspect ratio up to 28) scores
  `min_sicn = 0.1493`, i.e. it passes `>= 0.1` with 49 % margin and *no* margin at all if
  the layer is one refinement thinner. This is the intended reading of the metric, and the
  reason `edge_ratio` is **not** gated for prisms: gating the aspect ratio of a boundary
  layer would reject the layer for doing its job.

## 5. Gate policy added

| kind | checks | measured margin on `prism_plate_layer` / `pyramid_cube_fan` |
|---|---|---|
| `prism` | `min_sicn` ≥ 0.1 (non-positive ≤ 0), `scaled_jacobian` ≥ 0.1, `min_angle` ≥ 10° | sicn 0.1493 (49 %), sj 0.7071, angle 45.0° |
| `pyramid` | `min_sicn` ≥ 0.1, `scaled_jacobian` ≥ 0.0, `min_angle` ≥ 10° | sicn 0.8018, sj 0.5774, angle 54.74° |

`io.load_spec` and `report.measure` now validate node counts against the single table
`metrics.NODES_PER_CELL` (`prism: 6`, `pyramid: 5`); both previously carried their own
literal dictionary, and the CLI reads the same table. A kind the mesh does not contain
still yields no checks (vacuous, not an error), and a mesh whose only cells are prisms is
gated on prisms.

## 6. Limitations, stated rather than hidden

* **No parity against `gmsh`/`meshmagick` binaries.** Neither imports on this host and
  neither is installable from the configured index. The definitions were transcribed and
  checked by a second route; the parsed-upstream comparison remains open, exactly as
  `docs/mesh-quality-lib.md` records for the triangle set.
* **Pyramid `min_sicn`/`scaled_jacobian` sample 5 points, not Gauss points or the apex.**
  A metric that sampled the singular apex would be constant zero; sampling at the base
  corners plus the base centre is the choice documented in §2, not a universal convention.
  A pyramid that is badly shaped *only* near the apex can therefore look better here than
  a Gauss-point metric would report.
* **Volume is the conforming tet split**, which is exact for planar faces (all cells a
  conforming mesher produces) and is the volume the decomposition actually uses. For a
  deliberately warped (non-planar) base — `WARPED_PYRAMID` in the tests — the two
  diagonals give different splits; the metric reports the `0-2` diagonal, and the test only
  asserts agreement with the reference route on the *same* split.
* **Non-orthogonality is not extended to prisms/pyramids.** `topology.non_orthogonality`
  still accepts only `hex`/`tet`; the face-pair tables for the two new kinds are a
  follow-up, not covered here.
* **FD-route precision is 6e-10, not 1e-15, on degenerate cells.** The collapsed prism
  shows `max |lib−2nd| = 6.2e-10` while every other mesh is ≤ 2e-11: a finite difference
  of a map whose volume element goes to zero loses relative precision. That is why the
  acceptance is 1e-6 rather than 1e-9, and why the tests use `abs=1e-9` on the Jacobian
  columns and `abs=1e-12` on volumes/angles.
