# Righting arms (`GZ`) from station sections (0.23.0)

`tensorcad.stability` gives `GM`, the slope of the righting-arm curve at zero heel, and
says in its own docstring that it does not give the curve. `tensorcad.righting` gives the
curve from **station section areas**: each station is cut once, the section polygon is
clipped against the heeled waterplane, area and first moments come from Green's theorem,
and the stations are summed with Simpson at constant displacement. The two angles a naval
architect reads off the curve come with it (`GZ_max` and its angle, the angle of vanishing
stability).

## Public surface: this is the third route, and the three do not shadow each other

`main` already carries two answers to the same question -- `tensorcad/gz.py` (0.21.0:
divergence theorem on the rotated closed surface, waterline by **Newton** on the projected
wetted area) and `tensorcad/righting_arm.py` (0.22.0: per-face clipped sections in the body
frame, waterline by **bracket-and-bisect** on the monotone `V(h)`). PR #52 was cut from
0.20.0, so it arrived wanting to publish `righting_arm_curve`, `RightingArmCurve` and
`wall_sided_righting_arm` for the third time; merging it as delivered would have left one
route unreachable from the package surface while its own tests kept passing on a module
import. The cure (m4534, `docs/m4534_pr52_merge_cure.md`) keeps all three routes and gives
this one the `section_`/`Section` prefix:

| package name | module |
|---|---|
| `righting_arm_curve`, `RightingArmCurve`, `wall_sided_righting_arm` | `tensorcad/gz.py` (first publisher keeps the plain name) |
| `bisection_righting_arm_curve`, `BisectionRightingArmCurve`, `barge_wall_sided_righting_arm` | `tensorcad/righting_arm.py` |
| `section_righting_arm_curve`, `SectionRightingArmCurve` | `tensorcad/righting.py` (this page) |
| `submerged_body`, `ImmersedBody`, `RightingArmPoint`, `RightingError` | `tensorcad/righting.py` (no collision) |

`tests/test_righting.py::test_package_aliases_point_here_and_do_not_shadow_the_other_routes`
asserts every row of that table from the package, and
`test_barge_arm_agrees_with_the_other_two_routes_to_round_off` puts the three on one barge:
they agree to **7.9e-14 m**, because a box is a polyhedron and only the waterline solver
differs.

```
GZ(phi) = (y_B - y_G) cos(phi) + (z_B - z_G) sin(phi)
```

## Method

The body is heeled, not the water. Expressed in body coordinates the still-water surface
is the plane `s(y, z) = z cos(phi) - y sin(phi) = offset`, with the immersed side being
`s <= offset` -- the sign that puts the starboard side deeper for `phi > 0`. Each station
of the mesh is cut once with `x = station` (`tensorcad.bonjean.section_loops`), the
resulting polygon is clipped against the tilted half-plane, and its area and first moments
come from Green's theorem:

```
A          = 1/2 SUM (y_i z_(i+1) - y_(i+1) z_i)
INT y dA   = 1/6 SUM (y_i + y_(i+1)) (y_i z_(i+1) - y_(i+1) z_i)
INT z dA   = 1/6 SUM (z_i + z_(i+1)) (y_i z_(i+1) - y_(i+1) z_i)
```

which is *exact for the polygon the clip produced*. The station quantities are then
integrated along the hull with composite Simpson, giving the heeled volume, `y_B` and
`z_B` in one pass. The `offset` that holds the displacement equal to the upright
displacement is found by bisection on a bracket taken from the body's own vertical extent,
so no initial guess enters and `GZ(0)` comes out as round-off zero.

Composite Simpson is exact for this body when each lofted ring span holds two Simpson
intervals: doubling `station_intervals` from 80 to 160 changes the displacement by less
than `1e-9` relative, which is what `tests/test_righting.py` pins.

## Verification

Two references, neither of which is this module:

1. **The wall-sided closed form** `GZ = sin(phi)(GM + BM tan^2(phi)/2)` for a rectangular
   barge, exact up to deck-edge immersion. The barge is a polygon and the clip is exact, so
   the agreement is round-off: **5.8e-15 relative** over 0-45 degrees. Any sign error in
   the half-plane, the moments, the bisection or the lever formula lands far outside that.
2. **The initial slope from independent integrations**: `KB + BM - KG` with `BM = I_T/V`
   from `tensorcad.stability.waterplane_moments` (which cuts the *surface*, not the
   sections). On `sin^0.7 sqrt` hull with 0.3 m freeboard the recovered slope is within
   **5.5e-05 relative** of it.

The station-area volume also equals the closed-surface tetrahedron volume of the same mesh
(:func:`tensorcad.hydrostatics.compute_hydrostatics`) to **0.0e+00
relative** -- with the waterline at the cap, the two integrations must agree, and they do.

Worked numbers, the convergence ladder and the figure are in
[docs/righting/report.md](righting/report.md) (generated by
`examples/righting/run_example.py`, which exits non-zero if one of its eight claims stops
holding); the raw table is [docs/righting/results.json](righting/results.json).

## Scope -- read this before quoting a number

* **The mesh must include freeboard.** Every other module in the package treats the mesh
  as the immersed solid capped at its waterline, and `compute_hydrostatics` therefore
  returns the whole solid's volume whatever `waterline_z` says. `GZ` needs the opposite:
  body above the waterline to heel into. Use a negative `waterline_z` on a hull capped at
  its design waterline (giving `freeboard = -waterline_z`), or supply a mesh lofted to the
  deck. A waterline at or above the top of the body is **rejected**, not answered: with no
  freeboard every heel angle floods the deck.
* **The displacement quoted is the section integral below `waterline_z`**, not
  `compute_hydrostatics`' volume, for the reason above.
* **Deck-edge immersion is flagged, not hidden.** `RightingArmPoint.deck_immersion_fraction`
  and `RightingArmCurve.deck_immersion_onset_deg` report where the low-side deck edge goes
  under (the per-station `(B/2) tan(phi) > freeboard` condition). Past that angle the
  geometry is still integrated correctly -- the body is closed -- but the answer describes a
  watertight box, not a ship taking water over the deck edge. The barge curve shows both
  regimes: exact against the closed form to
  45 degrees, then 0.1414 m maximum at
  45.0 degrees that the closed form cannot represent.
* **Sections are assumed single-valued in `z` per `y`** (no overhangs, no immersed deck
  area inside the section), which is what a monohull below its deck edge is.
* **No free-surface correction, no tanks, no flooding.** Subtract
  `sum(rho i_x)/displacement` yourself, exactly as with `GM`.
* **Not a statutory criterion.** An angle of vanishing stability computed here is geometry.
  Damage stability, weathertight integrity and IS Code 2006 / SOLAS assessment are outside
  the module, and nothing here should be read as compliance.

## API

```python
import tensorcad as tc

curve = tc.section_righting_arm_curve(
    mesh,                    # closed mesh **with freeboard**
    kg=0.75,                 # centre of gravity above the keel (required keyword)
    waterline_z=-0.3,        # upright waterline; freeboard = 0.3 m here
    heel_angles_deg=tuple(float(a) for a in range(0, 91, 5)),
    station_intervals=80,
)
curve.gz_at(30.0)                 # linear interpolation, fails rather than extrapolating
curve.gz_max, curve.angle_gz_max_deg
curve.angle_vanishing_stability_deg, curve.deck_immersion_onset_deg
curve.to_dict()                   # JSON-ready; non-finite values become null

tc.submerged_body(mesh, heel_deg=10.0, waterline_z=0.0)   # the integral underneath

# the same three quantities inside the module, including the closed form this page
# verifies against (the package-level `wall_sided_righting_arm` is `gz`'s identical one)
from tensorcad.righting import wall_sided_righting_arm
wall_sided_righting_arm(30.0, gm=0.3, bm=0.2)             # the reference closed form
```

## Where it agrees with the other two routes (0.23.0, measured on this tree)

`tools/verify_merge_m4534.py` writes `artifacts/m4534/merge_verification.{json,png}`:

| check | measured |
|---|---|
| barge, three routes, worst `|dGZ|` over 0-45 deg | `7.9e-14 m` |
| barge vs wall-sided closed form, worst relative | `2.4e-14` |
| `41x13` hull at `z=-0.3 m`, section vs `gz`/Newton, worst `|dGZ|` | `7.3e-07 m` |
| station-area volume vs closed-surface volume (same cap) | `0.0` relative |
| 40 vs 80 station intervals, `GZ_max` relative | `4.8e-04` |
| negative control: `KG + 5 mm` moves `GZ(20 deg)` by | `1.7e-03 m` (2300x the inter-route gap) |

The hull gap is not round-off, and it is not claimed to be: the two routes discretise the
body differently (station grid versus triangles), so `~1e-7 m` on arms of order `1e-2 m` is
`~1e-5` relative -- four orders inside the 15 % band the platform treats as unacceptable.
