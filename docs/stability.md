# Initial stability: KM and GM from the waterplane moments

`hydrostatics.py` stops at the buoyancy side of the question and
`docs/hydrostatics_verification.md` says so in as many words: *"no metacentric height"*.
That was the last open item of the hydrostatics backlog, and it is the first question a
naval architect asks of a hull, because everything about loading it depends on the answer:

    GM = KB + BM - KG,     BM = I_T / volume,     I_T = int y^2 dA over the waterplane

`KB` was already integrated from the mesh; `BM` needs the waterplane's second moment,
which needs a plane section, which needs the surface cut rather than sampled.
`tensorcad.stability` is that cut, and this page is what it measures.

Everything on this page is produced by `python examples/stability/run_example.py
--output docs/stability`, which writes `results.json`, `report.md` and three figures, and
**exits non-zero if one of its ten claims stops holding**. The numbers below are read from
that JSON, not typed from a different run; `tests/test_stability.py` pins 19 cases of the
same content.

## How the section is measured

The waterline is not a sampled field and not a trapezoidal station sum. The surface is cut
with the plane `z = waterline_z`:

* each triangle is classified by the signed height of its three vertices, with vertices
  within `1e-9 × max(bbox side)` of the plane snapped *onto* it (a hull generated by
  `generate_hull` puts its waterline ring at exactly `z = 0`, so the tolerance is there for
  scaled or re-exported models);
* the intersection of a triangle with the plane is a segment with at most two distinct
  endpoints, keyed on the **mesh's own identity** -- the vertex index when a vertex lies on
  the plane, the sorted edge index pair when the point is interpolated along an edge. The two
  faces that share that vertex or edge therefore produce *bit-identical* endpoints, and the
  segments chain into loops with no geometric tolerance anywhere;
* faces lying wholly in the plane (a hull's waterline cap, a box's flat top) are counted and
  **skipped**: their boundary comes from the neighbouring non-coplanar faces. That is what
  keeps the area from being counted twice, which is the failure mode a naive `z <= waterline`
  filter walks into.

Each closed loop is then integrated by Green's theorem,

| quantity | integral | discretised as |
|---|---|---|
| `A` | `1/2 ∮ (x dy − y dx)` | `1/2 Σ (x₁y₂ − x₂y₁)` |
| `∫y² dA` | `−1/3 ∮ y³ dx` | `−(1/12) Σ (x₂−x₁)(y₁³+y₁²y₂+y₁y₂²+y₂³)` |
| `∫x² dA` | ` 1/3 ∮ x³ dy` | ` (1/12) Σ (y₂−y₁)(x₁³+x₁²x₂+x₁x₂²+x₂³)` |

which is **exact for the polygonal waterplane the mesh actually has**. The section is then
referred to its own centroid before the moment is used,
`I_T = ∫y²dA − A·y_c²` and `I_L = ∫x²dA − A·x_f²`, because `BM = I/∇` requires the moment
about the axis through the waterplane centroid -- the parallel-axis omission is the classic
error here, and on an asymmetric or heeled section it is not small. Nested loops (a waterline
through a twin-hull's tunnel) are detected by point-in-polygon parity and subtract, and the
two-loop case is a test, not a claim.

Three numbers come out of one meshing: the section itself, `BM = I/∇`, and
`KB = VCB − z_keel` from the existing volume integration. `KG` is an **input** -- it is a
loading condition, not a property of the shape -- which is why it is a required keyword
rather than a default of zero that would silently produce `GM = KM`.

## The box: the integration is exact, not merely converged

A `10 × 2 × 0.8 m` box, `KG = 0.55 m` above the keel, 12 triangles:

| quantity | measured | closed form | relative error |
|---|---|---|---|
| waterplane area | 20.000000 m² | `L·B` = 20 | `0.0` |
| `I_T` | 6.666667 m⁴ | `L·B³/12` = 6.666667 | `0.0` |
| `I_L` | 166.666667 m⁴ | `B·L³/12` = 166.666667 | `−2.2e-16` |
| displacement | 16.000000 m³ | `L·B·T` = 16 | `0.0` |
| `KB` | 0.400000 m | `T/2` = 0.4 | `0.0` |
| `BM_T` | 0.416667 m | `B²/(12T)` = 0.416667 | `2.2e-16` |
| `GM_T` | 0.266667 m | `T/2 + B²/(12T) − KG` | `0.0` |

Worst relative error **2.2e-16**, i.e. one ulp: the waterplane integration is exact
arithmetic on the section it is given, and any error that appears on a curved body is the
mesh's, not the integrator's. That distinction is what makes the hull table below
interpretable at all.

## The hemisphere: two independent integrals that must add up

A hemisphere of radius `R` cut at its flat face is the one body where `KB` and `BM` both have
a closed form and are computed by *different code paths*: the volume integration
(`KB = 5R/8` above the keel) and the waterplane moment (`BM = I/∇ = (πR⁴/4)/(2πR³/3) = 3R/8`).
Their sum is `KM = R` exactly -- the transverse metacentre of a hemisphere sits at the centre
of the sphere -- so a shared bug in one path cannot survive the check. Measured at
`n_lat = 32`, `n_lon = 64` (`R = 1 m`):

| quantity | measured | closed form | relative error |
|---|---|---|---|
| waterplane area | 3.136548 m² | `πR²` = 3.141593 | `−0.161 %` |
| `I_T` = `I_L` | 0.782879 m⁴ | `πR⁴/4` = 0.785398 | `−0.321 %` |
| `KB` | 0.625075 m | `5R/8` = 0.625 | `+0.012 %` |
| `BM_T` | 0.374624 m | `3R/8` = 0.375 | `−0.100 %` |
| `KM_T` | 0.999699 m | `R` = 1 | `−0.030 %` |

The residual is the inscribed `n`-gon: `−1.14 %`, `−0.285 %`, `−0.0714 %` of the area at
`n_lon = 24, 48, 96`, i.e. one quarter per doubling, which is the `O(1/n²)` signature of a
chord approximation and is asserted as such in the tests.

## The hull: second order in the grid, and where `GM` gets its error

The parametric hull of `docs/hydrostatics_verification.md` (`10 × 2 × 0.8 m`, breadth taper
`sin(πx/L)^0.7`) has an analytic waterplane, `|y| ≤ (B/2)sin(πx/L)^0.7`, so both moments have
references:

    I_T = (B³L/12) · taper_integral(3e)                                    (closed form)
    I_L = 2 B L³ ∫₀^{1/2} u² cos(πu)^e du,   u = x/L − 1/2                 (quadrature)

Note the exponent in `I_T`: the *half breadth* tapers as `sin^e`, the moment integrates its
cube, so the Beta integral carries `3e = 2.1` and not `e`. That is a mistake no mesh
convergence study would ever reveal -- both sides would be wrong together -- which is why the
reference is derived from `math.gamma` rather than mirrored from the integrator, and why `I_L`
is computed by **two unrelated rules**: tanh-sinh (double exponential, for the integrable
`cos(πu)^e` endpoint at `u = 1/2`) and composite Simpson after the substitution `u = 1/2 − s²`
that removes the endpoint singularity into a smooth `s^{2e+1}`. They agree to **7.8e-16**
relative, so the reference is rule-independent and the table below measures the mesh only.

`KG = 0.6 m` above the keel, `∇ = 7.512698 m³`, `BM_T = 0.435360 m`, `KM_T = 0.915360 m`:

| `nx × nz` | `A` [m²] | err `A` | `I_T` [m⁴] | err `I_T` | `I_L` [m⁴] | err `I_L` | `KB` [m] | `BM_T` [m] | `GM_T` [m] | err `GM` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 21×9 | 14.0062 | −0.568 % | 3.2524 | −0.559 % | 75.488 | −1.799 % | 0.4853 | 0.4411 | 0.3263 | **+3.47 %** |
| **41×13** | 14.0617 | −0.175 % | 3.2662 | −0.139 % | 76.406 | −0.604 % | 0.4830 | 0.4390 | 0.3216 | **+1.99 %** |
| 81×25 | 14.0787 | −0.054 % | 3.2696 | −0.034 % | 76.719 | −0.198 % | 0.4811 | 0.4366 | 0.3177 | **+0.74 %** |
| **161×41** | 14.0840 | −0.017 % | 3.2704 | −0.009 % | 76.822 | −0.064 % | 0.4805 | 0.4359 | 0.3165 | **+0.35 %** |
| 321×81 | 14.0856 | −0.005 % | 3.2707 | −0.002 % | 76.855 | −0.020 % | 0.4802 | 0.4356 | 0.3158 | **+0.13 %** |

Fitted orders (`log|error|` against the grid step `1/(nx−1)`): `I_T` **2.01**,
`A` 1.70, `I_L` 1.62, `∇` 1.32. `I_T` is cleanly second order because the waterline is
integrated on the chords themselves; `I_L` and `∇` carry the additional error of the lofted
sides, whose *first* derivative is what enters them.

**`GM` is not as accurate as `KM`, and the report says so instead of hiding it.** `GM` is a
difference of two nearly equal numbers, so its relative error is amplified:

    rel(GM) = (KM / GM) · rel(KM) ≈ 2.87 · rel(KM)   at KG = 0.6

At the default grid the `KM` error is 0.71 % and `GM` shows 1.99 %; on the coarsest rung
(21×9) `GM` is **3.47 %**, i.e. outside the 3 % band, while `I_T` itself is still inside it at
0.56 %. The claim table therefore binds `GM` at 3 % on the default grid and 1 % at 161×41, and
the coarse rung is printed as a measurement rather than promoted to a claim. A heavier
loading makes it worse and a lighter one better: a design that needs `GM` to 3 % near
`KG → KM` should be run on the fine grid, which is the recipe
`docs/mesh_conditioning.md` already gives for consuming a mesh at all.

The loading limit falls straight out of the same identity: `GM = 0` at `KG = KM = 0.915360 m`,
which the `GM(KG)` ladder on `gm_ladder.png` brackets between `KG = 0.9 m` (`+0.0165 m`) and
`KG = 1.0 m` (`−0.0836 m`). `GM` falls exactly one-for-one with `KG` (`dGM/dKG = −1` to
round-off), which is a test.

## What this is **not**

- **No free-surface correction.** `GM` here is the *solid* `GM`; a slack tank contributes
  `Σ(ρ·i_x)/Δ`, which the caller who knows the tankage must subtract. The report's `basis`
  string says `solid GM` so a consumer cannot miss it.
- **No large-angle stability.** `GM` is the slope of `GZ(φ)` at `φ = 0`. `GZ` needs a heeled
  displacement integration per angle (a different computation, not a waterplane moment), and
  no righting-arm curve, area under it, or statutory criterion (`IS Code 2008`, damage
  stability, weather criterion) is computed or claimed here.
- **No trim or longitudinal strength.** `GM_L` is reported (`≈ 100 × GM_T` on this hull) and
  is the stiffness that governs trim, but nothing here solves a trim equilibrium or a
  bending moment.
- **`KG` is not measured.** It enters as data. A `GM` printed here is only as meaningful as
  the loading condition that produced the `KG`.
- **The reference describes one analytic surface.** `KB`'s closed form (`0.6 T` above the
  keel, i.e. `−0.4 T` from the waterline, for the `√` vertical profile) and the two moment
  formulas apply to this hull family. A real ship needs its own offsets -- which the module
  will integrate happily, with no reference to compare against except a finer mesh.
- **The waterline must be a real section.** Asking for a waterline above the deck raises
  `StabilityError` instead of returning `A = 0`, because `A = 0` becomes `BM = 0` and then a
  confidently wrong negative `GM` two calls later.
- **A cap sitting exactly on the waterline is skipped, not integrated.** That is correct (its
  boundary supplies the loop) but it means a body whose *only* geometry at the waterline is a
  coplanar sheet -- a vessel modelled as a zero-thickness deck with no side above the
  waterline -- has no section. The case raises rather than guessing.

## Reproduction

```bash
python examples/stability/run_example.py --output docs/stability     # ~6 s, exits non-zero on a broken claim
python -m pytest -q tests/test_stability.py                          # 19 tests
```

`results.json` holds the box and hemisphere rows, the full ladder with every error, the fitted
orders, the quadrature cross-checks and the `GM(KG)` ladder; `report.md` is the same content
rendered as markdown; `sections.png` draws the waterline chords the mesh actually has against
the analytic planform, `convergence.png` the `|error|` against the grid step, and
`gm_ladder.png` the `GM = 0` loading limit. matplotlib is optional -- the JSON and the markdown
are written without it.

# Second route: the moments from the closed-surface flux identity

The module grew two independent integration routes and this merge keeps both, because
that is the cheapest cross-check available: `Hydrostatics` obtains `I_T` and `I_L` from the
flux identity over the closed wetted surface (`examples/stability/run_example_hydrostatics.py`),
while `waterplane_moments` cuts the surface with the plane and integrates the section
(`examples/stability/run_example.py`, everything above). On the default hull the two
routes agree to round-off wherever they use the same mesh, and both are measured against
the same closed forms -- the numbers in the two reports below are the same to three
decimals, from two different integrations.

The page below is the m2925 documentation of the flux route and of the analytic
reference, kept verbatim apart from demoted headings and the artifact file names, which
had to be separated so that neither example overwrites the other's results.

The hydrostatics module has reported displacement, areas and centres since 0.4.0. It
had no waterplane **second** moments, so the one number a naval architect asks for
first — the metacentric height — could not be produced, and every page in this
repository said so (`docs/hydrostatics_verification.md`, README "what this is not").
This release closes that gap with the two integrals, and turns them into KB, BM, KM
and GM in `tensorcad.stability`.

It closes it with a reference attached, not just a formula: the same two integrals
are available in closed form on the analytic hull surface, so every mesh number has
something independent to be measured against.

### What is computed

| Quantity | Symbol | Where |
|---|---|---|
| transverse waterplane inertia, `∫ y² dA` about the centreline | `I_T` | `Hydrostatics.transverse_waterplane_inertia` |
| longitudinal waterplane inertia, `∫ (x-LCF)² dA` about the flotation axis | `I_L` | `Hydrostatics.longitudinal_waterplane_inertia` |
| height of the centre of buoyancy over the keel | `KB` | `InitialStability.height_of_centre_of_buoyancy` |
| transverse / longitudinal metacentric radius, `I/∇` | `BM_T`, `BM_L` | `…_metacentric_radius` |
| height of the metacentre over the keel, `KB + BM` | `KM_T`, `KM_L` | `…_metacentre` |
| transverse / longitudinal metacentric height, `KM - KG` | `GM_T`, `GM_L` | `…_metacentric_height` |

```python
from tensorcad import generate_hull, initial_stability

model = generate_hull()                      # L=10, B=2, T=0.8, 41x13
stability = initial_stability(model.hydrostatics, height_of_gravity=0.5)
stability.transverse_metacentric_radius      # 0.438618 m
stability.height_of_transverse_metacentre    # 0.921623 m
stability.transverse_metacentric_height      # 0.421623 m
stability.is_stable_in_roll                  # True
```

`KG` is an **input**. TensorCAD has no weight schedule, so any KG it derived would
be a guess wearing a result's clothes; the caller supplies the loading, the code
supplies the geometry.

### How the inertias are integrated off the mesh

No waterline cap is needed, and no polygon has to be reconstructed. For any closed
surface the flux of `F = (0, 0, f)` is zero whenever `f` does not depend on `z`, and
every wetted face lies below the waterline, so

```
∫_{waterplane} f dA  =  - ∫_{wetted} f n_z dA
```

With `f = y²` this is `I_T`; with `f = x²` it is the moment about the origin, which
the parallel-axis theorem converts into `I_L` about the centre of flotation:

```
I_L = ∫ x² dA - A_w · LCF²
```

This is the same identity the module already used for the waterplane *area* (with
`f = 1`), so the waterplane quantities stay consistent with each other by
construction, including on a surface whose cap is missing.

The face integrals are exact: `x` and `y` are linear over a triangle, so

```
∫_face f² dA = A/6 · (f₁² + f₂² + f₃² + f₁f₂ + f₂f₃ + f₃f₁)
```

and `face_normal` is twice the area, which makes the accumulated `n_z · ∫f²` a
single multiply. The only error left in the result is the discretisation of the
*surface*, which is what the reference measures.

Two honest limits:

* Faces that straddle the waterline are not sliced (the same restriction the rest of
  the module has), and
* an **open** surface returns `nan` for both inertias rather than a finite number
  that would only reflect where the shell happens to stop — the flux identity needs
  a closed surface to hold at all. `basis` says `open surface (volume not
  trustworthy)` in that case, and `initial_stability` raises.

The sign of the accumulated flux is folded out instead of assumed, so a mesh built
with the opposite winding still reports non-negative moments.

### The reference on the analytic surface

The generating surface has half-breadth `b(x) = (B/2)·sin(πx/L)^e` with `e = 0.7`
(see `docs/hull_analytic_reference.md`), and the waterplane is the band
`|y| ≤ b(x)`. Both integrals collapse:

| Quantity | Closed form | Method |
|---|---|---|
| `I_T` | `(2/3)·(B/2)³·L·∫₀¹ sin(πξ)^{3e} dξ` | gamma identity — exact |
| `I_L` | `B·L³·∫₀¹ sin(πξ)^e (ξ-1/2)² dξ` | tanh-sinh quadrature, self-reported |
| `KB` | `0.6·T` (`VCB = -0.4T`, keel at `-T`) | exact |
| `BM_T`, `BM_L` | `I_T/∇`, `I_L/∇` with `∇ = B·L·T·I·2/3` | exact ratio |
| `KM_T` | `0.6·T + BM_T` | exact |

The longitudinal integrand keeps the `ξ^{-0.3}` endpoint of the taper, so it goes
through `tanh_sinh_quadrature` and carries `value_at_previous_level` with it; the
difference between levels 6 and 7 is `0.0` in float64, i.e. the reference is exact
to the resolution of the mesh values compared against it. The transverse one did not
need quadrature at all: `∫₀¹ sin(πξ)^{2.1} dξ` is the same gamma identity at a
different exponent, and `tests/test_stability.py` checks it against a direct
integration of `∫(2/3)b³ dx` to `1e-12` relative.

### Verification

`python examples/stability/run_example_hydrostatics.py` runs the resolution ladder
`21×9 … 321×81`, compares six quantities against the closed forms, writes
`docs/stability/{results_hydrostatics.json,report_hydrostatics.md,plot.png}` and exits non-zero if a claim
fails. Measured on the default hull (L=10, B=2, T=0.8):

| Quantity | 41×13 (default) | 161×41 | 321×81 |
|---|---|---|---|
| `I_T` | −0.139 % | −0.009 % | −0.002 % |
| `I_L` | −0.604 % | −0.064 % | −0.020 % |
| `BM_T` | +0.748 % | +0.127 % | +0.046 % |
| `KM_T` | +0.684 % | +0.120 % | +0.043 % |
| `GM_T` (KG = 0.5 m) | +1.508 % | +0.263 % | +0.095 % |
| `∇` | −0.880 % | −0.136 % | — |

Every quantity is inside 1.5 % at the default grid and inside 0.3 % at 161×41, so
the 3 % acceptance band holds with room to spare. The table is regenerated by the
example; `docs/stability/report.md` is the same table as emitted by the run.

`GM_T` is the weakest of the six, and for a structural reason worth stating: `BM_T`
is `I_T/∇`, so it inherits the *volume* error with the opposite sign. The mesh
under-integrates the volume by 0.88 % and the transverse inertia by only 0.14 %, so
`BM_T` comes out about 0.75 % high. A user who wants GM to 1 % needs a grid where
the volume error is also near 1 % — 81×21 in this ladder.

Two more checks live in `tests/test_stability.py`, and they are the ones that would
catch a *systematically* wrong identity rather than a discretisation error:

* a **rectangular barge** built vertex by vertex has `I_T = L·B³/12`,
  `I_L = B·L³/12`, `KB = T/2` and `GM = BM_T` when `KG = KB`. All four come out to
  `1e-12` relative on three different tessellations, including a single-element box
  — the flux identity is exact on a flat waterplane, so any leftover would be a bug
  in the bookkeeping, not in the physics.
* a **circular waterplane** (vertical cylinder) approaches `πR⁴/4` from below as the
  segment count doubles, with the error ratios falling by 4 each time (the `O(1/n²)`
  of an inscribed `n`-gon) and no face of it being horizontal-but-for-the-bottom.

### What this is not

Even keel, upright, small angles, one displacement, no free-surface correction, no
damage case, no `KG` model, no righting-arm curve, no IMO or class-rule criterion.
`GM_T > 0` is necessary for roll stability at small angles and says nothing about
the large-angle range: a hull with a large GM can still have a poor range because
the deck edge immerses early. The righting-arm curve needs `GZ(φ)`, which needs the
buoyancy centroid at heel, which needs waterline slicing of the mesh — the next
piece of work, not this one.
